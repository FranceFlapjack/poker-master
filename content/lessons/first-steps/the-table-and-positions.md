A hold'em table seats up to nine or ten. One small white disc sits in front of one player: the **button**.
It marks who would be dealing, and it moves one seat to the left after every hand.

Everything about the order of play is measured from the button.

```table
seats: 9
stacks: 25000
blinds: 100/200
button: 6
hero: 0
hand: As Kh
names: You, Ana, Bo, Cy, Dee, Eli, Fay, Gus, Hal
caption: Fay has the button. Gus posts the small blind and Hal the big blind — the two seats to the button's left. You are first to act.
```

## Who acts when

The two players immediately left of the button post the **blinds** — forced bets, before anyone has seen
a card. Small blind first, then big blind. The next lesson is about why.

Before the flop, action starts with the player to the big blind's **left** and moves clockwise. The blinds
have already put money in, so they act last.

After the flop — and on every later round — the order changes. It starts with the first player still in
the hand to the button's left, and the **button acts last**.

That change matters more than it sounds. From the flop onward, the same seat is last to act every single
round, for the whole hand.

## Why acting last is worth money

When it is your turn, everyone before you has already told you something. They bet, or they didn't. They
bet big, or small. You act knowing all of that. They acted knowing nothing about you.

That is the whole of position. It is not a subtlety — it is the single biggest structural edge available
at a poker table, and it costs nothing to use. The same two cards are worth playing on the button and
worth folding from the first seat, purely because of what you will know later in the hand.

The seats have names, and they are only shorthand for distance from the button:

- **Early position** — first to act, knows least. The hardest place to play.
- **Middle position**
- **Late position** — the cutoff (one right of the button) and the button itself. The best seats.
- **The blinds** — act last before the flop, first after it. That second half is the bad half, and it is
  why the blinds are difficult seats despite acting last preflop.

```try
type: choice
ask: Nine-handed. Before the flop, who acts first?
options: The button | The small blind | The player to the big blind's left | The big blind
answer: The player to the big blind's left
hint: The blinds have already put money in. Do they act early or late before the flop?
why: Preflop action starts to the big blind's left and runs clockwise, so the blinds act last. That is the one round where they have position on everybody.
```

```try
type: choice
ask: The flop comes down. Who acts first now?
options: The button | The first player left of the button who is still in | The player who bet last | The big blind
answer: The first player left of the button who is still in
hint: From the flop onward the button always acts last. So who has to go first?
why: On every round after the flop, action starts left of the button and the button acts last. If the small blind is still in the hand, that is them — which is why the blinds are awkward seats: last to act once, first to act three times.
```

```try
type: choice
ask: The hand ends. Where does the button go next?
options: It stays put | One seat to the left | One seat to the right | To whoever won the pot
answer: One seat to the left
hint: Everyone should get the good seat equally often.
why: The button moves one seat left after every hand, so every player takes a turn in every position. That is also why the blinds reach you: they follow the button round.
```

+++ Read more: how position changes which hands you play

A hand like queen-ten is a good example. On the button, with everyone else already folded, it is a fine
hand to raise with: only two players are left behind you, and you will act last for the rest of the hand.

Put the identical two cards in the first seat at a nine-handed table and it is a fold. Eight players are
still to act behind you, any of whom may have something better, and if anyone calls, you will spend the
whole hand acting first with no idea where you stand.

Same cards. Opposite decisions. Nothing changed except where the disc was sitting.

This is why a starting-hand chart is never one list — it is a list per seat, and that is what Track 2 is
about.

## Remember

- The button marks the dealer's seat and moves one left after every hand.
- The blinds are the two seats to its left.
- **Before the flop:** action starts left of the big blind; the blinds act last.
- **After the flop:** action starts left of the button; the button acts last.
- Acting last means acting with more information. It is worth more than most starting hands.
